# Remedies programme, Easy and Medium: an adversarial review

Read-only review of the records in `evals/` and the raw traces in `~/mellum-runs/f6/` and
`~/mellum-runs/r1/`, against the overview Paul was given. Nothing was run on a pod, no model
session was started. Scripts and intermediates:
`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-pauleveritt-projects-pauleveritt-mellum-worker/3ca45139-7360-4366-bfbd-5fc31f4b696b/scratchpad/review/`
(`tab.py` recomputes every record; `segs.py` splits traces at each send-back; `cf.py` truncation
counterfactuals; `stats.py` Fisher exact and bootstrap; `port.py` held-port send-backs; `ctx.py`
context composition and timeouts; `phases.py` Medium per phase).

Labels: **measured** is read from records or traces; **inference** is a reading of measured
numbers; **conjecture** is a mechanism or a counterfactual that no run has tested. "Proxy tokens"
are the budget proxy's charge (`budget.spent.output_tokens`, compaction summaries included);
"trace tokens" are `run.json`'s `output_tokens` (assistant messages only). "Full" is 13 of 13
hidden checks; "strict" adds the project's own tests and the lint gate; "near-miss" is at least
12 of 13.

### Caveats on claim 2 (the count)

- **The 4.47x is a noisy estimate of a skewed distribution.** Bootstrap 95% interval for the
  median ratio: proxy **[2.55, 5.77]**, trace [2.46, 4.60], requests [1.64, 3.93]. The mean
  ratios are lower (proxy 3.63 [2.68, 4.85]; trace 2.91 [2.24, 3.72]). Base's proxy tokens run
  7,846 to 67,777 and 16 of 32 sessions compacted; the median sits among the compacting
  sessions. (measured)
- **Three six-session screens with the same code disagree with the count.** The go-or-no-go
  screen (same code, same pod, the hours before the count) gave 15,578 (2.05x); the F.6 screen
  15,830; the fixed screen 15,878. Drawing 6 of the count's 32 Base sessions gives a median at or
  under 16,000 only 4.6% of the time, and three screens all did. Something in the count's
  conditions cost more than in the screens, and it is not identified. The held-port send-backs
  appeared only in the count (8 of 32 sessions; 0 of 12 unfixed screen sessions, p = 0.08) and
  are one candidate. The count's verdict stands under its rule, but 4.47x should not be quoted
  as a property of Base. (measured; the cause is conjecture)
- **Rule choices that did not matter.** Including stalled sessions in the medians: Vanilla's
  7 stalls cost 6,879 to 12,501 tokens, 5 of 7 above its median; the stall is the last act after
  the work, not a cheap exit. Dropping r02 (checks never ran) or r27 (read the test) changes no
  clause. The 10 budget endings are all above Base's median, so a higher cap moves the mean, not
  the median. (measured)
- **Where strict and full part.** The 5 Base sessions at 13 of 13 without a strict pass all edited
  `tests/test_home.py` (`integrity_ok: false`). Five of the six Base sessions that edited it did
  so in the build phase, before any send-back; two Vanilla sessions (r21, r27) did the same and
  would also have failed strict had they passed the checks. So this is a background behaviour
  the longer Base session exposes, not something the send-back caused. (measured)
- **The compaction cost is a consequence of how the send-back continues the context.** Of the
  252,534 proxy-only tokens, 228,160 fall after the first settle (7 compactions before, 45
  after). The re-dispatch appends a message to a context that is already near the threshold of
  33,616 tokens (50,000 window minus 16,384 reserve), so the next segment compacts almost at
  once. The earlier turns' thinking is replayed in the payload (`reasoning_content` present in
  every assistant message sent), and at the moment of a re-dispatch it is the largest part of the
  payload (for example r11 at its third send-back: 85,893 characters of thinking, 38,167 of tool
  results, 22,602 of assistant text). Tool results are a median 29% of the payload. (measured)
- **The held-port send-backs are smaller than the earlier analysis said.** 12 send-backs were
  flagged for a held port, but only 6 were port-only; the other 6 also named a real code failure.
  The port-only segments cost 48,174 trace tokens (5.7% of Base, not 14%) and 68,385 proxy. In
  every case the traces show the session's own backgrounded server (`... app.py &`,
  `nohup ...`), so the attribution to the model's own process was right. (measured)

### Caveats on claims 4 and 5 together: the pairing is not cost-neutral

F.6's settle-only arm stalled in 12 of 24; stalled sessions cost a median 14,224 trace tokens and
4 of 12 still passed, settled ones 16,094 and 12 of 12 passed. The count's Base (settle plus
bounded) stalled in 0 of 32 and cost 26,028 trace / 33,994 proxy. Bounded alone adds nothing
(7,967 vs 7,842), but paired with the re-run it keeps alive a session that would otherwise have
died on a foreground server, and that session is then sent back. This cannot be separated from
the other differences between F.6 and the count (32,000 vs 64,000 cap; the grader-mirroring test
vs the blind 16-test one), so it is an inference, not a measurement. It means "bounded is free"
holds only in isolation.

### Caveat on claim 6: the mechanism, and what was not tested

The foreground-server guard's refusal text proposes `nohup ... >/tmp/mellum-server.log 2>&1 &`.
With the shown test on, the model launched the app in 23 of 24 sessions, the guard fired in 14,
and 5 of the 7 stalls were a `tail -f` on a log, one on that exact path. The stall is the guard's
own suggestion followed one step further. (measured; the causal reading is inference.) Two
confounds: the pairing was with show plus settle (`check/mediated-test`), never with settle-only;
and all of it is on the Mac. Alone, the guard fired in only 4 sessions, so it could have prevented
at most 4 of Vanilla's 4 stalls; 0 vs 4 at n = 24 is p = 0.11.

### Caveat on claim 7: showing lengthens the build, and the cap then censors

Showing the test makes the build phase longer: median first-segment trace tokens are 13,019
(show-only) and 14,678 (show plus settle) against 7,300 (settle-only) and 8,323 (Vanilla). At
F.6's 32,000 cap the show-plus-settle arm ended on budget 6 times; mediated-r02 was re-dispatched
with no budget left (a 0-token segment). So "show plus settle passes less than settle alone" (11
vs 16, p = 0.24) is partly a cap artefact. What is well supported: showing costs about 5,000 to
7,000 tokens of build, buys no checks over Vanilla (6 vs 2, p = 0.24), and fails the lint gate
(4 of its 6 full passes not strict; in R.1 12 of 18 for show plus settle plus bounded), the
finding being an unused import. The lint failures are a real quality cost of showing.

### Claim 3: Medium, read per phase

Medium runs four prompts as four separate agent invocations per session; each phase has its own
send-back cap of 5. (measured from `instance_start` rows)

- base-r01: phases 1 and 2 each had one send-back and ended green; phases 3 and 4 had **no
  send-back at all**, never settled, and spent 118,789 trace / 261,311 proxy tokens before the
  budget ended the run (20 compactions). This session's cost is not the re-run's; it is a build
  that never says done, which Vanilla also shows (vanilla-r03 phase 4: 142,469 trace, 21
  compactions, a syntax error, checks never ran).
- base-r02: phase 1 took 4 send-backs (64,032 trace) on
  `test_home_is_rendered_from_home_html_which_extends_base_html`, a mediated test with **no
  hidden counterpart** (Medium's hidden phase-1 checks are status, doctype, language, nav links).
  Phase 4 took 4 send-backs (94,350 trace), the last naming 10 failing tests, and then the phase
  ended with 4 of the hidden phase-4 checks still red. Either the fifth run went green while the
  hidden checks stayed red, or `runTest` threw and the `catch { return undefined }` path let the
  session end. The trace cannot tell which. (measured; the two readings are inference)
- base-r03: phase 1 hit the cap of 5 on `test_the_navbar_shows_the_brand_and_links...` (56,637
  trace), again a mediated-only requirement; phase 4 took 3 send-backs and ended with the same
  four hidden misses.

So about 120,000 of Base's 486,464 Medium trace tokens (25%) went to phase-1 send-backs on
requirements the oracle does not have, and the phase-4 misses were either never reached by a
settle or not caught by the phase-4 test. The right conclusion is not only "more spend is not
buying finished results"; it is that **on Medium the blind mediated test is miscalibrated in both
directions**, and that the no-settle phase is a cost both arms share. Per hidden check passed,
the arms cost about the same: Vanilla 407,635 / 25 checks = 16,305 proxy tokens a check, Base
772,184 / 41 = 18,834. (measured; "miscalibrated" is inference)

Uncommitted raw runs under `~/mellum-runs/f6/medium-screen/` (old caps, 120 requests / 64,000
tokens) hold 4 Vanilla (4, 10, stall, 8 of 18) and 3 Base (15, 13, 13) sessions, all but one
budget-ended. They agree with the final screen's direction and are not in any note.

### Other confounds across the programme

- **Host, caps and code differ across batches.** R.1 (Mac, 64 / 32,000, worker arm for
  handlers, grader-mirroring test, old AGENTS.md line) vs F.6 (A40, 64 / 32,000, `--arm vanilla`,
  mirroring test, new AGENTS.md line) vs the count (A40, 128 / 64,000, blind 16-test file). The
  overview's claims 5 and 6 come from R.1, claims 4 and 7 from F.6, claims 1 and 2 from the
  count; none of these arms can be pooled, and the launch check is not comparable across the
  AGENTS.md change (the F.6 note says so).
- **Budget censoring.** R.1's two mediated arms ended on budget in 20 and 12 of 24 sessions, so
  their 25,787 and 27,028 medians are censored at 32,000; the count lifted the cap and the median
  rose. Comparing a censored median to an uncensored one understates how much the cap was doing.
- **Vanilla's floor moves.** Vanilla's full passes across batches: 0/12, 0/12, 0/24, 2/24, 0/6,
  2/32; stalls 21 of 110 pooled (19%). Mean checks 9.88 to 11.33. A rule relative to Vanilla's
  median inherits this drift.

## 2. Conclusions

**Warranted.**

- The re-run at settle is the one unit with a large, repeated, significant effect on Easy's
  hidden grade (F.6 16 vs 2; the count 30 vs 2 full, 25 vs 2 strict, p = 4e-9). Enforcement beat
  persuasion for *these* units on *this* suite: the two prompt fragments and the shown test did
  not move the two checks; the re-run did.
- Bounded commands end the foreground-server stall by construction, and the data never
  contradict it (0 of 24, 0 of 6, 0 of 32).
- Evidence is narrow: one model, one suite, two checks, and the launch check was confounded by a
  wording change midway.

**Overreached or incomplete.**

- "Showing does worse" should be "showing costs more and buys nothing" (above). The pass-rate
  difference against settle-only is real; the pass-rate difference *added* to settle-only is
  within noise and partly the cap.
- "fg-server paired with the mediated test stalled 7" is real and has a mechanism, but it was
  measured only with the shown test, which drives server launches. It says the guard's suggested
  background form is the hazard, not that the guard cannot pair with the re-run.
- "Bounded about equal cost" is true alone and not shown in the pairing that matters.
- Bounded's 5 vs 0 full passes (p = 0.05) was a lead the rule did not catch; it has not been
  followed. On the count's Base it cannot be separated from the re-run.
- The "4.47x" is reported without its interval. Any sentence that says Base costs 4.5 times
  Vanilla should carry [2.55, 5.77] and the three discordant screens.

**Missing.**

- The compaction cost (23% of Base's charged output) is a property of continuing the same context
  into the send-back, not of the send-back itself. The notes record it; they do not say that it is
  where the proxy-trace gap comes from and that it is addressable without touching the test.
- On Medium the mediated test's calibration against the oracle is the open problem, and the
  phases that never settle are a shared pathology. Neither is in the Medium note.
- A settle that throws lets the session end (`catch { return undefined }` in `settleMediated`).
  This is a hole in "unweakenable": a hung or crashing pytest ends the gate silently. It cannot be
  ruled out as what ended base-r02's phase 4.
- The frozen-test exception aside, "the model cannot read the test" now holds (fixed screens:
  0 hunts, 0 path mentions), but the hand-over copy named in `MELLUM_MEDIATED_TEST` remains
  readable, as the settle-fixes note says.

## 3. Tweaks, ranked by expected effect

All counterfactual savings are upper bounds computed by removing segments wholesale from the
count's traces; a session cut short might have spent differently, and its outcome at the cut is
read from the failing set at that settle, not from the grader. None of these can be proven
without a new run. Every tweak below names no suite file, route or expected value.

**T1. Start the send-back on a fresh context (or a model-free compaction at settle).**
Mechanism: in `agent_before_settle`, before returning `continue: true`, call `ctx.compact()` with
a `session_before_compact` handler that supplies a ready summary built without a model call (the
task prompt, the list of files the session wrote, the send-back message), or equivalently drop
the pre-settle history the way the worker's mode-integrity drop does. Pi exposes both
(`compact(options)` on the context, `session_before_compact` with `cancel`/custom compaction).
Saving (counterfactual): the 228,160 proxy-only tokens after the first settle, 21% of Base's
charged output; the proxy median falls toward the trace median, 33,994 to about 26,000 (4.47x to
about 3.4x); the trace median is untouched. Risk: the A12 ablation (2026-10-09) found that
stripping replayed thinking raised output tokens 73% because the replayed thinking is working
memory; a fresh context at settle keeps the thinking inside a segment and drops it only across the
settle boundary, which is a narrower cut, but the risk is the same kind. Smallest screen: Base vs
Base plus unit, n = 6 interleaved, reading compaction count and the proxy-trace gap per session,
not the median. Also test `context/trim-tool-output` in the same screen (tool results are 29% of
the payload at re-dispatch; a trim at 8,000 characters would cut the long `ps`/server-log reads
that dominate the port-hunting segments).

**T2. Stop the session's own processes before the re-run (shipped in `settle-fixes`).**
Saving (counterfactual from the count): the 6 port-only send-backs, 48,174 trace / 68,385 proxy
(5.7% / 6.2%); trace median 26,028 to 21,568 (2.84x), proxy 33,994 to 30,976 (4.08x) because the
median sessions are compacting title-fixers, not port-hunters. Pass risk: none; those segments
fixed nothing. The fixed screens show 0 held ports but at n = 6 cannot say what it does to a
32-session median, and the cleanup's "were stopped" line never appeared, so whether it was the
cleanup or chance that removed them is open. Smallest confirmation: a Base count at n = 32 with
the fixes, reading held-port send-backs (expect 0) and the proxy median.

**T3. Lower the send-back cap only after T2, and not below 2.** MAX = 2 on the count's traces:
trace median 23,067 (3.04x), proxy 31,614 (4.16x), 5 sessions cut, 4 of which passed strictly as
run; 3 of the 5 cuts were at a port-only send-back that T2 removes, leaving 2 cuts (r08 strict,
r12 not). So after T2, MAX = 2 is nearly free and nearly useless at the median. MAX = 1: proxy
23,876 (3.14x) but 10 sessions cut, 8 of them strict as run, and 7 of the 10 were cut on a real
code failure. I would not adopt MAX = 1. The n = 6 MAX 2 screen (1.82x, one fewer strict pass)
is consistent with this: a small saving and a pass at risk.

**T4. Make the send-back cheaper to act on.** After the first settle the model made 452
server-class calls (launch, curl, kill, ps, sleep) against 91 before it, 90 hunting calls (now
addressed by the hidden path and "this test is not in the project"), and 103 bounded timeouts in
19 sessions (about 51 minutes of waiting at 30 s each). The title fix is one block in one
template; a title-only send-back still costs a median 8,899 trace tokens, more than a whole
Vanilla session, mostly re-reading templates and relaunching the server. Agnostic options:
(a) the fixed message already says the test is not in the project; add that the acceptance run
starts the app itself, so the model need not (persuasion, cheap to try); (b) `MELLUM_BOUND_SECONDS`
10 instead of 30 saves wall time only, 103 x 20 s; (c) pair with `context/trim-tool-output` as in
T1. Screen: count server-class calls and timeouts per send-back at n = 6.

**T5. Thinking on the send-back segments.** 97 to 98% of generated characters are thinking in
both arms; the send-back segments are 98%. Lowering the thinking level for re-dispatch segments
only (`ctx.setThinkingLevel`) is the largest multiplier on the table and the least understood.
It is an ablation of the model's setting, not a remedy, and the 2026-10-20 ablation and A12 both
warn. Screen only after T1 and T2, and record it as an ablation.

**T6. Pairings not to make, from the traces.** Do not pair the re-run with the shown test (T7 in
the overview's terms: more build cost, lint failures, no checks). Do not pair the
foreground-server guard with anything that drives server launches unless its refusal text stops
proposing a log file, or `guard/foreground-log-follow` is on beside it (its screen could not judge
it at n = 3 because nothing launched; with the mediated test on, 5 of 24 sessions did `tail -f`,
so a pairing screen of fg-server plus log-follow plus settle-only would see the hook act).

**T7. Medium: calibrate the blind test against the roadmap's acceptance, and cap the phases that
never settle.** 25% of Base's Medium trace went to phase-1 send-backs on two requirements the
oracle does not test. The agnostic fix is in the hand-over discipline, not the code: a mediated
test asserts only what the roadmap states as acceptance, at the roadmap's strictness. The
no-settle phases (base-r01 phases 3 and 4; vanilla-r03 phase 4) are where the biggest single
costs sit in both arms; `guard/turn-cap` (dormant, unmeasured) is the existing agnostic unit for
"calls tools with no end". Screen: Medium Base vs Base plus turn-cap at n = 3, reading whether the
phase settles. Also close the `catch { return undefined }` hole: a settle whose test run errors
should send the error back (bounded by the cap), not end the session.

**Work after the test is green.** By construction, none: a green settle ends the session. Work
after the *hidden grader* would have been green cannot be measured without re-grading each
settle, but its lower bound is the 6 port-only send-backs (5.7% of trace), and no other send-back
in the count named a test without a hidden counterpart. On Medium, the two phase-1 cases above
are the same kind of waste at 25%.

## 4. Cost per strict pass as the bar

Figures over the count's 32 sessions per arm (measured; intervals by bootstrap over sessions).

| Measure | Vanilla | Base | Base / Vanilla |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proxy tokens per strict pass (all sessions' tokens / passes) | 302,732 / 2 = 151,366 | 1,098,978 / 25 = 43,959 [31,681, 62,282] | 0.29 [0.00, 0.82]; in 12% of resamples Vanilla has no pass |
| Trace tokens per strict pass | 145,568 | 33,858 [25,613, 46,155] | 0.23 |
| Requests per strict pass | 390 | 81 [59, 111] | 0.21 [0.00, 0.58] |
| Proxy tokens per full pass | 151,366 | 36,633 | 0.24 |
| Proxy tokens per near-miss (12+) | 15,137 (20 passes) | 35,451 (31) | 2.34 [1.50, 3.47] |
| Requests per near-miss | 39 | 65 | 1.67 [1.07, 2.41] |
| Median proxy tokens among strict passes | 8,315 (n = 2) | 21,552 (n = 25) | 2.59, no estimate at n = 2 |
| Median requests among strict passes | 17.5 (n = 2) | 43 | 2.46 |
| Base sessions above k x Vanilla's median proxy tokens | | 2x: 24 of 32; 3x: 18; 5x: 14; 6x: 8; 8x: 6 | |
| Expected tokens to the first strict pass, retrying failed sessions | 151,366 | 43,959 | 0.29 |

**What it gets right.** Per strict pass is the only measure here that prices what Paul said he
wanted to buy: a finished spec. The 3x median rule compares a Base session that finishes (25 of
32) with a Vanilla session that does not (2 of 32), and it rises exactly because Base does the
work Vanilla skips. Per pass, Base is a third of Vanilla's cost with a comfortable interval, and
the same ordering holds on trace tokens and requests.

**What it gets wrong or hides.**

- *Vanilla's denominator is 2.* 151,366 is not an estimate; 12% of bootstrap resamples have no
  Vanilla pass and the ratio is undefined. The bar must not depend on Vanilla's per-pass figure
  when Vanilla has fewer than, say, 5 passes. Use an absolute ceiling instead in that case.
- *Zero passes (Medium) gives infinity for both arms* and says nothing. The companion is cost per
  hidden check gained over Vanilla, or per near-miss; on Medium Base buys checks at about the
  same unit price as Vanilla (18,834 vs 16,305 proxy a check) and no finished result.
- *It hides the tail.* The average over sessions is indifferent to whether the spend is spread
  evenly or concentrated in runaways. 8 of 32 Base sessions cost more than 6 times Vanilla's
  median (14 of them more than 5 times); 10 ended on the budget cap and would have gone further.
  An unattended user pays for each of those; a reviewer sees each one. The companion is a
  per-session ceiling: a 90th percentile, or a share of sessions over k times Vanilla's median.
- *It is gameable in two ways.* A unit that ends failing sessions early lowers cost per pass
  without changing passes (an efficiency, but one to watch when a unit's effect is only on the
  failures). A unit that raises passes by naming the suite's content raises passes cheaply; the
  agnosticism rule, not the metric, is the guard. The near-miss companion catches a third abuse:
  an arm that trades many 12-of-13 sessions for a few 13s looks good per strict pass and bad per
  near-miss, which is what Base does here (2.34x per near-miss).
- *Reviewed or unattended changes which measure is primary.* If a person reviews each session's
  output, Vanilla's 20 near-misses at 15,137 a piece are useful work and per near-miss is the
  fair price (Base 2.34x). If the output must be finished unattended, only strict passes count and
  per strict pass is fair (Base 0.29x). The bar should say which setting it prices.
- *It is post-hoc.* The count was decided under a 3x median rule fixed beforehand; adopting
  per-pass after seeing the result is a new decision and has to be recorded as one, dated, with
  the count's verdict left standing and the per-pass numbers given as "what the new bar would
  have said". The rule note already has the shape for this (its 2026-11-09 correction).

**The bar I would write down.** For a counted Easy run of n = 32 per arm: Base passes if (1) it
has at least 8 more strict passes than Vanilla; (2) its proxy output tokens per strict pass, over
all 32 sessions, are at most Vanilla's, or at most 50,000 when Vanilla has fewer than 5 strict
passes; and (3) at most 10% of its sessions exceed 5 times Vanilla's median proxy tokens. State
whether the run prices unattended finishing (this bar) or reviewed output (replace strict with
near-miss in 2 and set the ceiling at 2x).

On the count, Base meets (1) 25 vs 2, meets (2) 43,959 against both 151,366 and 50,000, and
**fails (3)**: 14 of 32 sessions (44%) exceed 37,992, and the 90th percentile is about 66,000,
8.7 times Vanilla's median. That is the honest shape of the result: Base is cheap per finished
spec and has a fat tail of sessions that run to the cap. The tail is exactly where T1 and T2 act,
so a count with those in place is the run that would decide it; the per-pass figure should be
reported with its interval, and the tail clause with the count of sessions over the ceiling.

## What I think the overview got wrong or understated, in one list

1. The 4.47x is quoted without its interval [2.55, 5.77], and three same-code screens at
   1.8x to 2.1x are not reconciled with it. The count's conditions differed from the screens' in
   some way not yet identified; the held-port send-backs (count only) are the one candidate.
2. The compaction cost (23% of the charge) is caused by continuing the context into the
   send-back and is addressable without touching the test or the model.
3. Only half the held-port send-backs were pure environment; 5.7% of Base's trace, not 14%.
4. "Bounded is cost-neutral" is true alone and unshown in the pairing; paired with the re-run it
   keeps alive sessions that would have stalled.
5. "Show plus settle does worse than settle" is within noise and partly cap censoring; "show
   buys nothing and fails lint" is the supported statement.
6. The fg-server stall with the mediated test has a mechanism (its own suggested log file
