# F.6: one held pod, four Easy arms, and a screen

## For Andre

- **What this is:** F.6's proof run. One rented NVIDIA A40 held start to finish, 96 counted Easy
  sessions as an unprivileged user, then a six-session screen. No preregistration and no rule: the
  tables are measured, the reading is a judgment and says so.
- **The re-run at settle is the half that earns it.** Sending the model back on a red external
  test gave 16 of 24 full passes, against Vanilla's 2. Showing the test without the re-run gave 6.
  Doing both gave 11, and six sessions ended on budget.
- **The remaining loss is the foreground server.** 44 of the 96 sessions stalled and 42 of those
  ended on a command starting the app, with a line against it in AGENTS.md.
- **What to look at:** the screen at the end, a lead at n = 6: the re-run plus `bounded` settled
  all six and passed five.

**What is measured.** 96 counted sessions plus one speed-check session, 2026-11-08 14:45 to 20:57
UTC, and 6 counted screen sessions to 21:16 UTC. Pod `g5my3h9kfd9x9g`, NVIDIA A40, Secure Cloud,
$0.59/hour, llama.cpp commit `cd959bae`, Pi 1.4.0, commit `26b17d60`. Conformance passed all 7
items ([host card](../../../evals/2026-11-08-f6-easy/host-card.json); GPU-against-CPU numerics
drift 0.24%). Records: [batch](../../../evals/2026-11-08-f6-easy/table.md),
[screen](../../../evals/2026-11-08-f6-screen-settle-bounded/table.md). Each session's
[`mediated.json`](../../../evals/2026-11-08-f6-easy/settle-only-r01/mediated.json) records the
sha256 of the test it had, and `run.json` the three settings that vary a remedy.

**What is conjecture.** Why showing the test does not help (below); anything about Medium or Hard;
anything from the screen beyond the lead it is.

## Ladder run

`scripts/r1_batch.py run scripts/pod/easy-arms.json 24`, round-robin, `--arm vanilla`, 64 requests
and 32,000 output tokens, 180 s stall, resumable. The setup is
[`scripts/pod/`](../../../scripts/pod/bootstrap.sh) and `learn/code/pod.py`. One speed-check
Vanilla session took 73 s including grading, 13 requests, against about 3 minutes on the Mac, so
the run stayed on the A40. No session was contaminated or failed its record.

| Arm (n = 24) | Full passes | Settled / stalled / budget | Title override missed | Launch missed | Median requests | Median output tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| vanilla | 2 | 18 / 6 / 0 | 22 | 9 | 22 | 8,910 |
| `check/mediated-test` | 11 | 4 / 14 / 6 | 11 | 3 | 32.5 | 15,573 |
| `check/mediated-test-show` | 6 | 8 / 12 / 3, 1 error | 16 | 9 | 28 | 13,019 |
| `check/mediated-test-settle` | 16 | 12 / 12 / 0 | 5 | 5 | 33 | 15,532 |

Medians here are over all 24 sessions, read from the records; the
[batch table](../../../evals/2026-11-08-f6-easy/table.md) and its per-check table leave stalled
sessions out of the median. No arm's median is near the 64-request cap, but the mediated arm's
median over non-stalled sessions, 32,245 output tokens, sits on the 32,000 cap and is censored.

Full pass means all 13 checks. The stricter `passed` also needs the project's own tests and the
lint gate, and the arms shown the test failed that gate in 22 of 48 sessions against 0 of 48
otherwise. The commonest finding in those sessions is an unused import in `app.py` (19 findings,
16 of them `HTMLResponse`).

**What the traces show.**

- All 12 settle-only sessions that settled passed in full, against 4 of the 12 that stalled.
- A re-dispatch never exceeded 2. Re-dispatched: 18 settle-only and 10 mediated sessions.
- 42 of 44 stalls ended on a server start. The other two, a mediated and a settle-only session,
  ended on `grep -R` over `/` for the name of the test that had just failed; neither returned.
- No session's tool calls named the handed-over test directory.

**The AGENTS.md line, and what it does to comparison.** Before these runs the suites' AGENTS.md
said "Do not: start servers", while the roadmap requires `uv run --frozen python app.py` to start
uvicorn with reload and says to check the work "never by starting a server". The line now reads
"Do not start a server to check your work; run the tests." It was changed because the grader's
launch check tests the entry point, and R.1's overnight showed the blanket line could be costing
it: of the sessions that missed the launch check, those that never launched the app were Vanilla
4 of 6, bounded 4 of 4 and fg-server 11 of 12, against 0 of 3 and 0 of 5 in the mediated arms,
which launched the app in 23 and 16 of 24 sessions. The prompt points the model at AGENTS.md, and
21 of 24 Vanilla sessions in that overnight read it. **So these runs are not comparable with R.1's
on the launch check.** No technique was added, so the floor gets no quiet help beyond the wording.

**Limits.** Pod numbers do not pool with the Mac's. Sessions ran as `mellum-session`, but grading
ran as root; the old branch's separate grading user was not ported. The speed comparison is one
session.

**Judgment (a judgment, not a rule).** The re-run at settle is what carries the mediated test: it
cut title-override misses from 22 to 5 and launch misses from 9 to 5, at about 1.5 times Vanilla's
median requests and 1.7 times its output tokens, and no session ended on budget. Showing the test
did not pay: alone it missed the title override 16 times and the launch 9, the launch no better
than Vanilla, at more cost than Vanilla and with the lint failures above. Added to the re-run it
lowered full passes from 16 to 11 and brought budget endings from 0 to 6; at n = 24 the 16 against
11 alone is within noise. My reading of why is
conjecture: the model works toward the shown test's text and writes more around it. Whether
the cost is worth paying is Paul's call against R.1's budget limit, which no arm here would meet.

## The screen: settle-only + bounded (n = 6, a lead)

`--remedy tool/bounded-commands,check/mediated-test-settle`, 30 s bound, serial after the batch,
same settings, its own [directory](../../../evals/2026-11-08-f6-screen-settle-bounded/table.md).

| Arm | n | Full passes | Settled / stalled / budget | Median requests | Median output tokens | Misses |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| settle-only (batch) | 24 | 16 | 12 / 12 / 0 | 33 | 15,532 | title 5, launch 5, others 1 or 2 each |
| settle-only + bounded | 6 | 5 | 6 / 0 / 0 | 31.5 | 15,830 | bootstrap script 1 |

Every session was sent back once. The one miss, r05, is a real defect: its `home.html` left a
`<div>` unclosed, so the script stopped being the last element of `<body>`.

Three of the six started the app, and none hung. When a start was cut off, the model read the
error and moved on: r03, cut at its own 10 s, later backgrounded the server, was cut at 30 s, then
wrote a TestClient script; r05 used `timeout 10`, then backgrounded it and curled; r06 was the
noisy one, four 30 s cut-offs and several wrong commands before reading uvicorn's import-string
warning and fixing the main guard, and it used the most requests (46). A cut-off costs wall time,
not requests.

A lead, read beside 12 stalls in 24: bounded removed the stalls without visible added cost, and
did not stop the model trying to start the server.

## Persuasion → enforcement

| Move | Kind | What it did here |
|---|---|---|
| AGENTS.md "Do not start a server to check your work" | persuasion | 42 of 44 stalls were still server starts |
| Showing the test (`before_agent_start`) | persuasion | no better than Vanilla on the launch; dearer |
| Re-running the test at settle (`agent_before_settle`) | enforcement | 16 of 24 full passes, 2 for Vanilla |
| Bash timeout on every call (`tool_call`) | enforcement | 0 stalls of 6, a lead |

## What would falsify this / Risks

- The re-run's gain is partly by construction. A repository test requires a mediated test for every
  hidden Easy check, so the re-run sends the model back on what the grader judges. 16 of 24 shows a
  complete external test gets obeyed, not that the build is better by an independent measure.
- The show-against-settle reading rests on one batch at n = 24 on one suite, without a rule.
- A hidden-grader change, or a different roadmap, could move the title override and launch checks,
  which carry most of the difference.
- The re-run uses a mediated test written from the roadmap; on a suite whose test tracks the oracle
  less closely, it could send the model after the wrong thing.
- The screen's six sessions came last on a warm server and share no round with the batch.
- A filesystem-wide `grep` for a failed test's name is a search for the grader. Here the traverse-only
  directory held and neither search returned, and that is the isolation working, not evidence that
  a less bounded host is safe.
