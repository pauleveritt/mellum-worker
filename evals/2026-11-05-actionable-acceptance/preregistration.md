# Actionable feedback: fixed follow-up comparison

**What is measured:** the preceding [LC.34 cohort](../../../evals/2026-11-05-protected-acceptance/README.md).
**What is conjecture:** preserving actual startup failure evidence and clarifying
repair scope will improve bounded repair success.

This registration precedes all follow-up inference. Collect twenty fresh tasks
per arm, sequential S,G alternation, with the original
[protocol](2026-11-04-protected-acceptance-protocol.md) limits, scoring,18/20
candidate threshold, output-per-usable rule and unknown/no-decision rule.
S keeps the same five selective remedies; G adds only protected acceptance.

Two recorded feedback changes distinguish this G implementation: bounded,
labelled untrusted launch diagnostics, and generic current-task cleanup/revert
instructions. No acceptance criterion is weakened, and no suite answer enters
the generic remedy. Their individual effects cannot be separated by this study.
All prior outcomes and qualification costs remain separate. Forty outcomes are
required; no favorable stopping, replacement of model failures or mid-run edits.

Freeze this protocol, old protocol, implementation, package, image and the same
M5 Max/backend/profile before counting. Qualify runtime warning transport,
valid builds, mutations, hostile data, actual Pi repair/exhaustion and compaction.
Keep all failed request/repair costs. Candidate output is untrusted data and
cannot confer acceptance. Hidden grading remains independent and unseen.

An eight-hour independent watchdog, full600s plus60s reserve per new task,
180s open-tool cutoff and identity-checked cleanup remain. Stop inference on
finish or failure, verify independently, retain raw locally and do not upload
or publicly push without explicit publication approval.

**What would falsify the lead:** fresh qualified results fail to improve usable
outcomes, repeat the same ignored feedback, or expose valid artifacts rejected
by the checker. A new measured defect requires a dated new freeze.
**Risks:** one repeated task, stochastic variation, combined feedback changes,
semantic proxies and unknown interrupted token usage. Passing does not establish
multi-phase or cross-task trust.
